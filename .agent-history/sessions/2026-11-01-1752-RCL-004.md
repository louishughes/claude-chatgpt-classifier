### 17:52 — RCL-004: `recall decisions history` and `recall decisions conflicts` (#416)

**What:** The last ticket of the recall slice. Two read-only commands for one project:
- `recall decisions history NAME` groups the project's decisions by subject and prints each subject's steps oldest first: suggested, accepted, rejected, clarified, revised, replaced, contradicted. Every record in a subject quotes its message, with chat, message ID and date.
- `recall decisions conflicts NAME` lists the pairs that still disagree, both sides quoted, marked "unresolved: nothing says which holds". `--all` adds the ones a person already dismissed or resolved.

The new module is `src/knowledge_engine/recall/decisions.py`. It decides nothing itself. The steps are the REC-004 timeline (the append-only `decision_events`), each record's standing comes from the REC-006 projection through the same document builder as `recall projects show`, and the conflicts are the REC-003 register. A "subject" is the records joined by any event: a suggestion and the decision that took it up, a decision and what replaced it.

Status words follow the approved mapping: proposed (an answered suggestion), explored (a suggestion nobody answered), confirmed (a decision or requirement holding now), preferred (a preference), and rejected, superseded, conflicted, pending and "date unknown" as before. A step a reviewer withdrew stays on screen, marked "revoked by WHO: WHY". `recall/projects.py` gained one public helper (`check_quote`, the quote re-check it already did privately) so both modules check quotes the same way.

**Louis's decisions:** none new; the status mapping is his, from the approved plan.

**Teach-back (`recall/decisions.py`, chosen by the thread under blanket authority):** This shows how a project's decisions moved, oldest first: who suggested something, whether I accepted or rejected it, and what later replaced or contradicted it, with a quote for every record. It never decides a replacement itself; it only shows the ones the engine recorded for an explicit switch, so a later decision about something else never counts as replacing an earlier one. Conflicts stay marked unresolved until a person settles them, and a step a reviewer withdrew is still shown, marked, so corrections are visible.
