### 02:15 — SOC-004: confirm or reject a social account, and link a bare @handle (#381)

**What:** the social account registry (SOC-002) allowed an account to be `confirmed` or `rejected`, and a mention to be `rejected`, but nothing ever wrote those values. Every account stayed `probable`, and a bare `@handle` could never be tied to anyone. Now:
- `ke accounts review ACCOUNT confirm|reject --reviewer NAME [--note]` sets the account's status.
- `ke accounts link MENTION ACCOUNT --reviewer NAME` files one ambiguous `@handle` mention under an account. It then counts toward that account and leaves the "needs review" list. `ke accounts --ambiguous` now lists the mention IDs to pass in.
- Every decision is a row in a new `account_reviews` table (migration 0034). Database triggers refuse UPDATE and DELETE on it, so the history can't be rewritten. A purge is the one sanctioned exception, as for the other review histories.
- A rejected account drops out of `ke accounts`, the Accounts page and the registry note. `ke accounts --all` still shows it, and its own page and note keep its mentions and a "Review history" section.

**Decisions (delegated by Louis's /goal: "use your own judgement"):**
- A person can change their mind. Reject and then confirm is allowed, and each step is its own history row. The account shows the latest one.
- Only an ambiguous mention can be linked, and never to a rejected account. Linking it again is refused.
- A linked mention keeps "no platform", because the chat still didn't name one. The person's decision is what ties it to the account.
- Re-importing can't undo a review. Imports only add accounts and mentions and never touch existing rows, and a test proves it.
- Reviewer names and notes are typed by a person, so the notes put them in code spans. That way they can't turn into Markdown links.

**Teach-back (`links/social_store.py`):** The registry keeps one row per social account, however the chats wrote it, and one row for every place it was mentioned. Only a person can confirm or reject who an account is, or say that a bare @name means a particular account. Each of those decisions goes into a history the database itself won't let anyone edit or delete.
