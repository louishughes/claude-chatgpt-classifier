# TAX-009: chats no domain fits become one proposal per subject

**When:** 2026-10-29 00:22 UTC. **Ticket:** TAX-009 (#371). **Thread:** build and merge.

**Why:** reviewers on corpus pass 1 kept finding chats with no domain, for example cooking and food, or garden and room makeovers. The classifier can use only approved terms, so these chats stayed unlabelled on every run, and nothing turned the repeated miss into a suggestion.

**What changed:**
- New `taxonomy/domain_gaps.py`, built around `propose_domains`.
  - A segment is *uncovered* when, under the run's version, no `done` answer gives it a domain and no reviewer chose one. A reviewer's "none fits" leaves it uncovered.
  - Uncovered segments are grouped by the word with the highest *lift*: its share of the uncovered segments divided by its share of all answered segments. A word the covered chats also use ("weekend") loses to one only the subject uses ("cooking").
  - These never count: stop words; words in more than half of all answered segments; words that look like a name (used mid-sentence and always capitalised there); and *bridge* words, which join subjects that other words already tell apart.
  - A group of at least 3 segments (`--min-segments`) becomes one TAX-004 `add` proposal for a new domain. Its evidence holds the segment IDs, one anchor per segment (message ID and span, never text), the run's taxonomy version and the rule version `tax-009-v3`.
- `ke taxonomy refresh` now runs it over every chat classified under the current version and reports `domains_proposed`.

**Design calls:**
- The common-word share is measured over the whole run, not just the uncovered segments. If it were measured over the uncovered segments only, a subject shared by most of the uncovered chats would be thrown out as filler.
- A label already approved, waiting or rejected is not proposed again. Neither is a group made mostly of an earlier open, rejected or approved proposal's segments, which needs at least half of the group and more than half of that proposal. In both cases the word is passed over without taking its segments, so the next word still gets a chance. A rejection is remembered through its proposal row, so no new table or migration was needed.
- It writes only proposal rows. The vocabulary still changes only through a separate reviewer and the owner's publish (NOTE-014).

**Teach-back (`taxonomy/domain_gaps.py`):** It finds the segments in a run that no domain covers and groups them by the word most of them share. When at least three share a word, it files one suggestion for a new domain, which points at the exact places that word appears. It never changes the vocabulary itself: a different reviewer approves or rejects the suggestion, and a rejected one doesn't come back.

Fix attempts: 1. At first I used the TAX-006 discovery backend, which measures "too common" within the uncovered segments only. That threw away a subject shared by most of the uncovered chats, so the share is now measured over the whole run.

**Clean review:** a reviewer with no context, given only the ticket's test conditions, found one blocker and five more points. Each fix has its own regression test.
- **Blocker:** cooking and garden chats that all said "weekend" became a single proposal, "Weekend". Rejecting it then hid both subjects, because their segments had already left the pool. Words are now ranked by lift, and a label or group that was seen before is passed over without taking its segments.
- A rejection was remembered by label only. It is now also remembered by group.
- The CLI measured word shares against the queue only, so after a pack a queue that was mostly one subject made that subject look like filler. Shares are now measured against every classified chat.
- The latest review now decides: a reviewer's "none fits" overrides an earlier domain.
- Privacy: `keywords` was removed from the evidence, and a recurring name is never used as a label.
- A `pending` segment no longer counts as uncovered.

**Second clean check:** it found two more blockers and two smaller points. Each fix has its own regression test.
- **Blocker:** "weekend" still merged cooking and garden when no covered chat used it, because lift tied and the larger group won. Such *bridge* words are now passed over. A word is a bridge when two or more other words each mark at least 3 of its segments, overlap each other by at most a third, and together cover two thirds of them. This works for two subjects and for three.
- **Blocker:** approved proposals now count as earlier ones, both by label and by group. Re-running after approving "Cooking", or approving it as "Food", proposes nothing for those chats.
- Reviews and labels are read for the run's taxonomy version only.
- The name guard now looks at mid-sentence uses only, so "…times. Bramble can visit" is still a name.

Fix attempts: 3 in total: the first build, the first review round, and this second one. That is the ticket's limit.

**Talking points:**
- "A word only counts if it's typical of the uncovered chats, so 'weekend' can't lump cooking and gardening together."
- "Chats my vocabulary didn't cover used to sit unlabelled. Now three or more on the same subject become one suggestion, with the exact places it came from."
- "Suggesting never changes the vocabulary mid-run. A person approves it, and the next version picks it up."
