# TAX-009: chats no domain fits become one proposal per subject

**When:** 2026-10-29 00:22 UTC (redesigned 01:40 UTC). **Ticket:** TAX-009 (#371). **Thread:** build and merge.

**Why:** reviewers on corpus pass 1 kept finding chats with no domain, for example cooking and food, or garden and room makeovers. The classifier can use only approved terms, so these chats stayed unlabelled on every run, and nothing turned the repeated miss into a suggestion.

**What changed (the redesign, `tax-009-v5`):**
- New `taxonomy/domain_gaps.py`, built around `propose_domains`.
- **Uncovered:** a segment that has an answer under the run's version but no domain. A pending segment doesn't count. The latest review under that version decides, so a reviewer's "none fits" overrides an earlier domain.
- **Each segment's words:** one tokenizer, which keeps letters and digits together (`covid19`). It leaves out:
  - stop words, filler, and a fixed list of about 200 everyday words ("time", "stop", "weekend");
  - stock sentences: any sentence of 3 or more words found verbatim in 3 or more chats, such as "Let me know if you'd like a printable checklist" or "Cheers in advance";
  - words in more than half of all answered segments (the DF cap);
  - words that no other uncovered segment uses.
- **Weights:** each word is weighted by IDF over the answered segments. A word the user wrote counts in full; a word only the assistant wrote counts half. The top 15 words are kept for each segment.
- **Groups:** two segments are linked when their IDF-weighted cosine is at least 0.4 **and** they share at least one word that both users wrote. An inverted index finds the pairs, so 2,000 segments link in under 1 s. A connected group of 3 or more becomes one TAX-004 domain `add` proposal.
- **Label:** chosen after the group exists. It is a word the users wrote, present in at least half of the group, with the highest lift; ties go to IDF-weighted frequency, then alphabetical order. A word that looks like a name (most mid-sentence uses capitalised, or never written in lower case) is skipped. If no word qualifies, the label is "Unnamed group N". A name always written in lower case can't be told from a word, so the label is only a suggestion: the reviewer can rename it with `approve(label=...)`. The module docstring says the same.
- **Never twice:** a group is passed over when its label, or mostly the same segments, matches an approved domain or an open, rejected or approved proposal. The match uses the proposal's full member list, which is stored in the evidence together with its SHA-256. The display list and the anchors stay capped at 50.
- **Evidence:** IDs, the member hash and message/span anchors, never text.
- **CLI:** `ke taxonomy refresh` runs it over every chat classified under the current version and reports `domains_proposed`. `--min-segments` now defaults to the module's `MIN_SEGMENTS`.

**Thresholds, chosen from the realistic probes:** same-subject pairs of realistic gardening, travel and money chats scored 0.46 to 0.94. The closest cross-subject pair (credit card vs travel card) scored 0.34. Without the shared-user-word rule, assistant replies that both said "water" and "warm" linked cooking to travel at 0.69, so that rule stays. A DF cap of 0.4 or a two-shared-words rule broke the earlier probes, whose chats share only one subject word.

**Design calls:**
- I chose grouping by whole-segment similarity over single words. One shared filler word ("weekend") is one word in three, so it can't link subjects. Chats that share two subject words ("cooking", "recipe") stay together, even when one bakes and one grills.
- **Known ambiguity:** some layouts are symmetric, and no similarity rule can split them correctly. Three "cooking bake …" and three "cooking grill …" chats look exactly like three "cooking … weekend" and three "garden … weekend" chats, so both give two groups. A 4×4 grid, where every subject chat also shares a variant word with one chat of each other subject, gives no group. Those chats stay uncovered and still sit in the NOTE-014 refresh queue.
- No migration: the full member list is stored in the proposal's evidence JSON.

**Teach-back (`taxonomy/domain_gaps.py`):** It takes the chats in a run that no domain covers and links any two that share enough of their distinctive words. Each linked set of three or more becomes one suggestion for a new domain, named after its most typical word, with pointers to where that word appears. It never changes the vocabulary: a different reviewer decides, and anything already approved or rejected isn't suggested again.

**History, the word-based design (budget used up):**
- **Attempt 1, the first build:** grouped by most-shared word.
- **Attempt 2, the first clean review:** found one blocker and five more points.
  - **Blocker:** cooking and garden chats that all said "weekend" became a single proposal, "Weekend". Rejecting it then hid both subjects, because their segments had already left the pool. Words are now ranked by lift, and a label or group that was seen before is passed over without taking its segments.
  - A rejection was remembered by label only. It is now also remembered by group.
  - The CLI measured word shares against the queue only, so after a pack a queue that was mostly one subject made that subject look like filler. Shares are now measured against every classified chat.
  - The latest review now decides: a reviewer's "none fits" overrides an earlier domain.
  - Privacy: `keywords` was removed from the evidence, and a recurring name is never used as a label.
  - A `pending` segment no longer counts as uncovered.
- **Attempt 3, the second clean check:** found two more blockers and two smaller points.
  - **Blocker:** "weekend" still merged cooking and garden when no covered chat used it, because lift tied and the larger group won. Such *bridge* words are now passed over. A word is a bridge when two or more other words each mark at least 3 of its segments, overlap each other by at most a third, and together cover two thirds of them. This works for two subjects and for three.
  - **Blocker:** approved proposals now count as earlier ones, both by label and by group. Re-running after approving "Cooking", or approving it as "Food", proposes nothing for those chats.
  - Reviews and labels are read for the run's taxonomy version only.
  - The name guard now looks at mid-sentence uses only, so "…times. Bramble can visit" is still a name.
- **After attempt 3, a third clean check:** still found failures. Bake and grill chats were split into two subjects. `covid19` crashed with an IndexError. A name used once in lower case became a label. The evidence cap of 50 broke rejection matching above 100 segments.

**Clean review of the redesign (fix attempt 2 of the fresh budget):** the realistic probes failed. Stock closers and sign-offs ("Let me know if you'd like … a printable checklist", "Cheers in advance") merged subjects. Three realistic gardening chats gave no proposal, because everyday words and words only the assistant wrote chained cooking, gardening and travel together. The fix is `tax-009-v5` above: stock sentences dropped, everyday words ignored, user words weighted ahead of assistant words, IDF-weighted cosine, a link needs a shared user word, a label must be a user word. New tests use realistic multi-sentence chats with stock closers: gardening, travel and money give Garden, Trip and Money with no mixing; closers and sign-offs merge nothing; linking 2,000 segments takes under 2 s.

**Known limit:** a lone chat that shares one distinctive user word with a group can join it. In a draft fixture with a single travel chat, "travel card" joined the three money chats through "card". With three travel chats it doesn't. The reviewer sees the members before approving.

**Talking points:**
- "Chats are grouped by how much of their wording they share, so one common word like 'weekend' can't lump cooking and gardening together."
- "Suggesting never changes the vocabulary mid-run. A person approves it, and the next version picks it up."
